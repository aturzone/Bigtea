Suppose a fair coin is tossed repeatedly and we record the sequence of heads and tails. Compute the expected number of tosses required before the pattern heads-tails-heads appears for the first time, and compare it with the expected waiting time for heads-heads-heads. Explain carefully why these two answers differ even though both patterns have the same probability of occurring in any three fixed consecutive positions. Present the correlation-polynomial argument in full, and then verify the result by setting up and solving the corresponding Markov chain with its absorbing state. Finally, generalise: describe how to determine, for any two distinct patterns of equal length, which one has the shorter expected waiting time, and give an example where intuition about symmetry misleads.
