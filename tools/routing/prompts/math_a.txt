Let f be a real-valued function that is twice differentiable on the closed interval from zero to one, with f of zero equal to zero and f of one equal to one, and suppose the second derivative of f is bounded in absolute value by a constant M on that interval. Prove that there exists a point c strictly between zero and one at which the first derivative of f equals one. Then derive an upper bound, in terms of M alone, on how far the first derivative of f can deviate from one at the endpoints. Discuss whether the bound is tight, and construct an explicit function that attains it or explain why none can. Finally, state what changes if f is only assumed to be once differentiable with a Lipschitz derivative rather than twice differentiable.
