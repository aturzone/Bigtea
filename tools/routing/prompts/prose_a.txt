The harbour had emptied by the time she reached the last pier, and the water held that particular grey that comes before rain rather than after it. She had rehearsed the conversation on the train, out loud once, quietly, until the woman across the aisle looked up. Now the sentences would not come in order. Her father had always said that the sea forgives nothing and forgets everything, which she had taken for wisdom at eleven and for evasion at thirty. The boats knocked against their moorings with a sound like someone patiently knocking on a door in another room. She sat down on the cold bollard and watched a gull work the length of the pier, unhurried, entirely certain of its business, and she thought about how little of her own life had ever been conducted with that kind of confidence.
