## Findings

### ktransformers contribution dynamics
- Repo: https://github.com/kvcache-ai/ktransformers — 19,079 stars, 1,489 forks, Apache-2.0, created 2024-07-26, last push 2026-07-24 (as of 2026-07-28).
- Sample of 40 most-recently-updated closed PRs (#1984–#2105, spanning 2026-05-07 to 2026-07-23): 32 merged / 8 closed-unmerged. Median time-to-merge across the 32 merged = **1.08 days**. Distribution is bimodal: release/version-bump/CI-sync PRs and PRs from a ~10-person repeat-contributor ring merge in 0.0–5.0 days; PRs from apparent first-time/outside accounts take 13.0–32.9 days (e.g. #2022/#2021 hermannklie, 32.9d; #2029 Oxygen56, 24.1d; #1984 login256, 13.0d) or get closed without merging after weeks (#2033 lphuc2250gma, 20.1d, closed not merged — "improve ktransformers maintenance path").
- Snapshot backlog: 16 open PRs, **456 open issues** (search API, 2026-07-28). 25 PRs opened / 10 merged in the trailing 30 days — small, actively-cycled PR queue vs. a large, slow-moving issue backlog.
- Issue triage sample (17 most-recent open issues, created 2026-06-11 to 2026-07-28): **9/17 (53%) have zero comments**, including a security-relevant report (#2042, unauthenticated `pickle.loads` over ZMQ, opened 2026-06-16, 0 comments) — though that class of bug was independently fixed via merged PRs #2059 ("HMAC-authenticate scheduler RPC", merged after 1.7d) and #2091 ("bind balance_serve scheduler ZMQ socket to loopack", merged after 1.2d), suggesting security reports get acted on even when the issue thread itself isn't answered. Older bug-report issues cited in the companion gaps doc (#1104, #1022, #1074, #109) were each closed same-day-to-within-days; #1173 (Windows Vulkan binary, feature request) has sat open ~15 months.
- Formal governance: `MAINTAINERS.md` (https://github.com/kvcache-ai/ktransformers/blob/main/MAINTAINERS.md) lists 11 named maintainers, all affiliated with Tsinghua MADSys Lab (https://madsys.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/) or Approaching.AI (http://approaching.ai/); explicit path to becoming a maintainer ("sustained, high-quality contributions and collaborative behavior"). Of the 38 non-bot PR authors in the 40-PR sample, only 1 (ovowei) is a listed maintainer — but a ~10-person ring (yyj6666667, Anai-Guo, callmegaga, VectorPeak, devangpratap, jdai0, Dayuxiaoshui, ovowei, etc.) handles ~76% of PRs with maintainer-speed turnaround despite not all appearing on the formal maintainers page — consistent with the project's private WeChat dev-group model (see below) creating a de facto inner contributor ring wider than the written governance doc.
- Roadmap/governance signal on tooling direction — **two roadmap issues found, Q1 supersedes assumption in task brief, Q2 is current**:
  - 2026 Q1 roadmap (https://github.com/kvcache-ai/ktransformers/issues/1779, 11 comments, opened 2026-01-04): lists "Develop KTransformers CLI tool" (model mgmt, bench, tune/search-params, quant, SFT) as an **assigned, in-progress core-team item** (@SkqLiao, dev branch `ljq-cli-dev` linked), plus "Improve installation experience" (@ovowei, checked done) and "Optimized expert offload strategy" (@ovowei, checked done). Ends with an open invitation: "Any contribution is welcomed, please email yzwliam@126.com / ervinxie@qq.com ... join our development WeChat group."
  - 2026 Q2 roadmap (https://github.com/kvcache-ai/ktransformers/issues/1921, 15 comments, opened 2026-04-09, most current — referenced from README): explicitly targets "**Deliver the best MoE inference performance on consumer-grade x86 + NVIDIA GPU**," "Optimize expert offloading and scheduling strategies" (@ovowei), and "**Establish recommended configurations for typical consumer hardware setups**" — i.e. the core team is already road-mapping work that materially overlaps two of Bigtea's three backlog epics (auto-tuning, hardware-profiling/config-recommendation). **No observability/per-expert-metrics/monitoring item appears in either Q1 or Q2 roadmap** — that gap area is not on their stated plan.
  - Net signal: maintainers explicitly solicit external contributors into a real (if informal, WeChat-mediated) collaboration channel, and have a track record of merging community PRs fast (median ~1 day) — but for two of the three tooling areas (auto-tuning, hardware-profiling) they are already building in-house, raising direct redundancy/competition risk for an independent effort in those two areas specifically. Observability is open, uncontested territory per the roadmap.

### Wrapper feasibility per gap (a/b/c per gap area, from ktransformers-vs-llamacpp-moe-offload-gaps.md)
- **Auto-tuning via `optimize_rule` YAML injection** — **(a) confirmed**. Injection tutorial (https://github.com/kvcache-ai/ktransformers/blob/main/doc/en/injection_tutorial.md) describes a static YAML file consumed by existing loader code; a wrapper can generate/select these files programmatically with zero source changes.
- **`kt-expert-placement-strategy` / `kt-num-gpu-experts` / `kt-gpu-experts-ratio` / `kt-enable-dynamic-expert-update` auto-tuning** — **(a) confirmed**, and more mature than the original gaps doc assumed: these are plain CLI flags to `python -m sglang.launch_server` (doc: https://github.com/kvcache-ai/ktransformers/blob/main/doc/en/kt-kernel/experts-sched-Tutorial.md). A wrapper can pick values from a hardware-profiling step and pass them straight through — no config/patch needed.
- **Per-expert observability — verified (a), stronger than assumed in the task brief.** The `experts-sched-Tutorial.md` documents first-class, already-shipped flags: `--record-kt-gpu-expert-distribution`, `--expert-distribution-recorder-mode {stat,stat_approx,per_pass,per_token}`, and env var `SGLANG_EXPERT_DISTRIBUTION_RECORDER_DIR=/path/to/output` to redirect recorded stats to a readable location. This is SGLang's own `ExpertDistributionRecorder`/EPLB machinery (confirmed independently: SGLang docs list `sglang:eplb_balancedness` as a public Prometheus Summary metric, flag `--enable-eplb`, plus separate flags `--expert-distribution-recorder-mode`, `--enable-expert-distribution-metrics`, `--expert-distribution-recorder-buffer-size` — https://sgl-project-sglang-93.mintlify.app/observability/metrics, https://docs.sglang.io/advanced_features/expert_parallelism.html). Since kt-kernel's `forward_binding` (kt-kernel/operators/moe-tp.hpp:165, and pybind surface in kt-kernel/ext_bindings.cpp:460-468) takes a pre-computed `expert_ids` buffer as an argument rather than computing routing itself, the gating/routing decision is made upstream in SGLang's own Python model code — meaning SGLang's expert-distribution recording almost certainly fires regardless of whether the FFN math executes on GPU natively or via kt-kernel's CPU/AMX path. **Net: log-parsing + CLI/env-var flags, zero patches required, for the routing/activation-frequency half of "per-expert observability."** One residual verification item: confirm end-to-end at runtime that kt-kernel's SGLang integration doesn't bypass this recorder (plausible given the pointer-passing architecture, not yet runtime-confirmed).
- **Cache-hit-rate for the CPU↔GPU expert-transfer path — mixed (a)/(b).** No existing named cache-hit/miss counter exists anywhere in the kt-kernel C++ tree (`git grep -i "cache_hit|cache_miss|hit_rate"` across kt-kernel/ returns only a hardcoded `const bool cache_hit = false;` test stub in `operators/amx/test/amx-test.cpp` — not real instrumentation). However the raw ingredients to compute it externally already exist and are exposed to Python via pybind11: per-expert GPU-residency is readable through `GeneralMOEConfig.gpu_experts_mask` / `.physical_to_logical_map` (kt-kernel/ext_bindings.cpp:741-746), and per-token routing is the same `expert_ids` buffer described above. For **static placement strategies** (`uniform`/`frequency`/`front-loading`/`random`, no dynamic update) cache-hit-rate is trivially computable externally by cross-referencing the SGLang expert-distribution log against the (unchanging) resident-expert mask — **(a)**. For **`--kt-enable-dynamic-expert-update`** (GPU-resident set changes mid-inference based on live routing stats during prefill), there is no confirmed externally-visible log of *when/which* experts get swapped — computing a true hit rate under dynamic updates needs either an upstream patch to emit swap events (**(b)**, small, and precedented — see below) or reverse-engineering timing from the `per_token` recorder mode, which is unconfirmed to be sufficient.
- **Batching/serving reliability (originally scoped as balance_serve fixes, e.g. issue #1074's KV-cache-loss bug)** — **reclassified, not (c).** `balance_serve` (the custom Python serving engine issue #1074 was about) is now archived: it lives only under `archive/ktransformers/server/balance_serve` and `archive/csrc/balance_serve` in the current tree, not the active codebase. As of README updates through 2026-07, the project's serving path has moved to being a **kt-kernel plugin consumed by SGLang** ("Oct 10, 2025: Integrating into SGLang," https://github.com/kvcache-ai/ktransformers, blog: https://lmsys.org/blog/2025-10-22-KTransformers/). Practically, "fix batching/serving reliability" now means working against **SGLang's** (much more mature, independently-resourced) batching/serving stack via its own config flags and `/metrics`, not patching ktransformers-specific code — **(a)**.
- **Install/build UX gaps** (CMake/CUDA-compiler-not-found, GLIBCXX version, Windows install.bat failures) — **(a) for mitigation, (c) for root fix.** A wrapper/preflight tool can detect and clearly report these failure modes (this is exactly Bigtea's separate hardware-profiling epic) but cannot make a broken upstream CMake/toolchain path succeed; genuinely fixing the build fragility requires upstream CMakeLists/install-script changes (**(c)**, though these are exactly the kind of small, self-contained patches that merge fast per the contribution-dynamics data above — e.g. #2074/#2071/#2070 were all CLI/build-detection fixes merged in 0.0–0.1 days).
- **Windows-native / AMD-perf / platform coverage gaps** — **(c)**, unavoidably. These require new native kernel code paths (AVX/AMX/ROCm/Vulkan variants) that only upstream (or a fork sustaining its own native code) can supply; a wrapper has nothing to inject.

### Precedent: wraps vs forks
- **llama.cpp forks:**
  - **koboldcpp** (https://github.com/LostRuins/koboldcpp) — true fork, relicensed AGPL-3.0 on top of MIT-licensed llama.cpp (permitted: MIT allows sublicensing under more restrictive terms provided the original MIT notice is retained for incorporated code). 11,284 stars, 742 forks, actively pushed same week as this research (2026-07-27). Faces recurring feature-parity lag: when upstream added MTP (multi-token-prediction) support, three MTP models reportedly wouldn't boot in koboldcpp (issue thread: https://github.com/LostRuins/koboldcpp/issues/2211) — direct evidence of fork-maintenance drag.
  - **ik_llama.cpp** (https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp) — detached fork (GitHub API reports `fork: false`, i.e. not a linked fork), MIT, 2,969 stars, 392 forks, single-maintainer (ikawrakow), pushed 2026-07-28. Genuinely ahead of upstream on quantization/CPU-perf (SOTA quant types, FlashMLA, fused MoE, row-interleaved packing; measured 0.05–0.15 perplexity-point improvements and CPU speed gains — DeepWiki summary: https://deepwiki.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/1.1-key-features-and-performance-improvements). Maintainer's own words on why he stays independent (https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/discussions/256): explicitly "I will not be upstreaming stuff to llama.cpp," motivated by wanting to avoid the support burden of a large non-technical user base, not by any stated technical merge obstacle. Some of his quantization work only began landing in mainline llama.cpp in March 2026, roughly a year-plus after divergence — i.e. even a "successful" fork's innovations take a long time to reach the wider ecosystem, and reconvergence happened only when upstream chose to port it in, not via the fork upstreaming.
- **llama.cpp wrappers:**
  - **ollama** (https://github.com/ollama/ollama) — 177,096 stars, 17,152 forks, MIT. Vendors a pinned llama.cpp commit (reported stuck at b7437 / Dec 2025) rather than tracking head; a live issue (https://github.com/ollama/ollama/issues/15601) reports a ~56% tokens/sec gap vs. standalone llama.cpp on Vulkan/AMD specifically because of this staleness — concrete evidence of the "wrapper falls behind and hits a capability ceiling" failure mode. Ollama's own response has been to build a **separate, non-llama.cpp inference engine** for multimodal models and (March 2026) an MLX backend for Apple Silicon — i.e. outgrowing the wrapped dependency rather than deepening the wrap (https://ollama.com/blog/multimodal-models, Ollama issue #9959).
  - **LocalAI** (https://github.com/mudler/LocalAI) — 47,956 stars, 4,295 forks, MIT; wraps llama.cpp plus several other backends via a plugin/binding architecture (breadth-over-depth wrapper model).
  - **ramalama** (https://github.com/containers/ramalama) — 2,976 stars, 353 forks, MIT; wraps llama.cpp (and others) at the container/CLI level — UX and distribution layer, not a performance-critical wrap.
  - Secondary-source framing worth noting (blog, lower confidence, not verified against primary sources beyond what's already independently confirmed in the gaps doc): https://www.banandre.com/blog/ollama-koboldcpp-are-doing-it-wrong-llamacpp-auto-memory-fit argues llama.cpp's new `--fit` auto-memory-tuning threatens to obsolete exactly the kind of manual-tuning wrapper tooling both koboldcpp and ollama built, and that downstream tools must "integrate llama.cpp's automation or become obsolete." The gaps doc independently found `--fit` still has multiple open correctness bugs (Windows overflow #20308, non-authoritative failure handling #22592, MoE-model slowdown #20492) as of mid-2026, so "solved" is contestable, but the strategic risk the blog names — upstream automation eroding a wrapper's value-add — is a real consideration for Bigtea's own auto-tuning tooling.
- **ktransformers forks specifically — no successful precedent exists.** Largest fork by stars is `vproxy-tools/ktransformers` at **45 stars** (vs. 19,079 on the original); every other listed fork is ≤9 stars, several are single-digit and dormant (checked via GitHub forks API). Notably, ikawrakow (the ik_llama.cpp maintainer) also forked ktransformers once (6 stars, last pushed 2025-04-11) with no sustained activity. No third-party "wrapper" project built around ktransformers-as-backend was found via web search either. **Unlike llama.cpp, ktransformers has no fork or wrapper ecosystem to learn from — Bigtea would be the first meaningful attempt at either path**, so risk/reward here is closer to a first-mover bet than a well-trodden pattern.

### Licensing mechanics
- ktransformers LICENSE (https://github.com/kvcache-ai/ktransformers/blob/main/LICENSE) is the **stock, unfilled Apache-2.0 template** — the appendix copyright line still literally reads `Copyright [yyyy] [name of copyright owner]`, never filled in. **No NOTICE file exists** in the repo. Spot-checked source files (e.g. `kt-kernel/operators/moe-tp.hpp`) carry no per-file copyright headers either.
- Practical fork obligations under Apache-2.0 §4 given the above: (1) include a copy of the License (trivial — copy the file as-is), (2) mark modified files as changed (real, ongoing obligation for any fork), (3) retain existing copyright/patent/trademark/attribution notices found in the Source form (currently ~none to retain, per the above), (4) reproduce NOTICE-file attribution content if a NOTICE file exists (N/A today, but a fork should re-check on each upstream sync since the maintainers could add one later). Overall compliance burden is low relative to typical Apache-2.0 projects specifically because ktransformers hasn't populated the boilerplate.
- Apache-2.0 §3 grants an express patent license from every contributor for their own contributions, terminating for any party that files patent-infringement litigation against the project over it — standard defensive-termination clause, relevant if Bigtea's fork later touches patent-sensitive kernel techniques (e.g. quantization methods).
- Apache-2.0 §6 explicitly does **not** grant trademark rights. No `TRADEMARK.md`/brand-policy file was found in the repo, but the project name is registered as a PyPI package (`ktransformers`) and has a logo/name used in its docs site and SIGOPS paper citation — a fork publishing under the identical name or logo risks trademark/passing-off exposure even absent a written policy; would need a distinct package/project name.
- **Wrapper obligations are materially lighter**: calling ktransformers as an external process/API (not linking/embedding/modifying its source) doesn't trigger Apache-2.0 §4's "state changes" or notice-retention duties at all — those only bite when you redistribute (portions of) the Licensed Work itself, modified or not. A pure wrapper's only real obligation is not misrepresenting itself as, or affiliated with, the ktransformers project.
- **llama.cpp is MIT** (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/LICENSE — filled-in: "Copyright (c) 2023-2026 The ggml authors"). Key differences from Apache-2.0 if Bigtea also targets llama.cpp as a second backend: MIT has **no patent grant/defensive-termination clause at all** (silent on patents — weaker protection, but also nothing to trip over), and **no "state changes" requirement** — MIT's only redistribution condition is retaining the copyright notice and license text in copies/substantial portions. Simpler to comply with, but offers contributors/forkers no patent assurance.
- Notable cross-cutting fact: **ktransformers itself already vendors llama.cpp as a git submodule** (`kt-kernel/.gitmodules` → `third_party/llama.cpp` pointed at `github.com/ggerganov/llama.cpp`), alongside its own forks of `sglang` (`third_party/sglang` → `github.com/kvcache-ai/sglang`) and `custom_flashinfer` (on branch `fix-precision-mla-merge-main`). This demonstrates in-tree precedent for (a) MIT code living inside an Apache-2.0-licensed project without friction, and (b) the maintainers' own working pattern of forking upstream dependencies and periodically syncing them — see next section.

### Hybrid option viability
- The maintainers already run the exact "hybrid" pattern being evaluated, on their own dependencies: they maintain forks of `sglang` and `custom_flashinfer` under `third_party/`, with a recurring, automated `[build] Sync sglang-kt submodule` PR merged every 1–2 weeks by `github-actions[bot]` (e.g. #2055, #2047, #2032 in the PR sample — merged same-day-to-5-days). This is a concrete, sustained, in-house example that "wrapper/fork-of-a-dependency + periodic upstream sync" is operationally viable at this project's scale, not just a theoretical hybrid.
- Given contribution dynamics above (median ~1-day merge for small, well-scoped PRs from repeat contributors; explicit "any contribution welcome" + WeChat-group onboarding path; two roadmap cycles in a row inviting outside help on "help wanted" items), **small upstream PRs for narrowly-scoped hook points look genuinely viable**, particularly for: (a) small CMake/install-script robustness fixes (precedented — #2074/#2071/#2070 merged in <1 day), and (b) emitting a dynamic-expert-swap event log to close the one real observability gap identified above (a small, additive, non-invasive change in the same spirit as the already-existing `get_profile_stats`/`reset_profile_stats` env-var-gated profiler pattern found in `kt-kernel/operators/amx/sft_moe.hpp` and `kt-kernel/python/sft/profiler.py`, which is itself precedent that maintainers accept opt-in instrumentation hooks bound through pybind11).
- Countervailing risk: two of Bigtea's three tooling epics (auto-tuning, hardware-profiling/config-recommendation) sit squarely inside the Q2 2026 roadmap items the core team has already assigned to named maintainers (@ovowei "optimize expert offloading and scheduling strategies," unassigned "establish recommended configurations for typical consumer hardware setups"). A hybrid approach here risks building a competing solution to something already in-house, or duplicated effort if timed to land around the same window — argues for either fast engagement via the WeChat channel to coordinate/deconflict, or deliberately concentrating independent effort on the observability epic where no roadmap collision exists.
- No sustained fork or wrapper of ktransformers has ever reached meaningful adoption (see Precedent section) — there's no existing hybrid-model case study specific to this project to validate against; the closest analogues are the llama.cpp ecosystem precedents above, where wrappers hit staleness/capability-ceiling problems (ollama) and forks hit feature-parity lag (koboldcpp) or single-maintainer bus-factor risk (ik_llama.cpp) even when the fork was technically superior.

## Open questions
- Whether kt-kernel's SGLang integration truly preserves SGLang's standard `ExpertDistributionRecorder` code path end-to-end at runtime, or short-circuits/bypasses it for the CPU-offloaded expert compute — inferred highly likely from the `forward_binding(expert_ids, ...)` pointer-passing signature (routing decided upstream in Python before reaching kt-kernel), but not confirmed by actually running a server and inspecting recorder output.
- Whether `--expert-distribution-recorder-mode per_token` output format/schema is documented anywhere beyond the flag's existence — not found in the tutorial; would need to run it or find source to confirm parseability.
- Whether a genuine cache-hit-rate metric under `--kt-enable-dynamic-expert-update` is derivable purely from `per_token` recorder output plus polling `gpu_experts_mask`, or whether dynamic-swap events need a dedicated log — not resolved; flagged above as the one area where a small upstream patch (category b) may be the cleanest fix rather than pure external computation.
- Whether the ~10-person "inner ring" of fast-merging non-listed-maintainer contributors (yyj6666667, Anai-Guo, callmegaga, VectorPeak, devangpratap, jdai0, Dayuxiaoshui) are paid contributors, WeChat-group members with informal write access, or just prolific unpaid regulars — GitHub profiles show no company affiliation for most, so this is inferred from merge-speed pattern only, not confirmed.
- No first-party or independent comparison found of contribution experience (PR-to-merge friction, responsiveness) between ktransformers and llama.cpp specifically — this analysis characterizes ktransformers alone; a relative baseline against llama.cpp's own governance/PR dynamics was out of scope here and would sharpen the ADR if needed.
